# A 104-Year-Old African Company Is Changing Hands. What Survives?

**Africa's Enduring Enterprises | Substack adaptation | Draft — not published | 2 November 2026**

Africa does not lack entrepreneurs. It needs more businesses that can become institutions and outlive the people who created them.

East African Breweries is a useful test. The company traces its beginnings to Kenya Breweries in **1922**. It became a public company, developed a regional business and survived several generations of owners and managers. Its corporate history records a 1936 merger and a 1954 Nairobi listing.

Today it is approaching another ownership transition. In December 2025, Diageo signed agreements to sell its indirect **65% controlling stake** to Japan's Asahi. Kenya's competition regulator gave conditional approval in September 2026. But a regulatory approval is not a completed share transfer: I have not independently verified closing as of 2 November.

There is an important capital-formation distinction here. Diageo estimated **US$2.3 billion in net sale proceeds**. That money would accrue to the selling group, not automatically become fresh investment capital for EABL. Asahi expects to preserve EABL's public listings and locally owned brands while entering licensing arrangements for Diageo's international brands.

The founding story itself also demands precision. EABL's heritage page gives **8 September 1922** as its incorporation date; Asahi's transaction disclosure gives **8 December 1922**. Both agree on the year. The original company registration should settle the exact date. This is why our longevity register distinguishes a company history from independently verified legal continuity.

A different kind of succession occurred in 2021, when Jane Karuku became EABL's Group Managing Director and CEO after leading Kenya Breweries. That professional leadership transition offers a counterpoint to the much larger ownership transaction.

The question is not simply whether a century-old brand survives a change of shareholders. It is whether its governance, management bench, supplier relationships and ability to invest survive—and improve.
