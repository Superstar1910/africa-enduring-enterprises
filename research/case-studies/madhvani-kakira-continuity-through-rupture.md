# Madhvani Group and Kakira: continuity through rupture

*Research brief | 19 October 2026 | Provisional evidence assessment*

## Thesis

The Madhvani Group is an example of a **persistent family enterprise lineage**, not proof that its principal operating subsidiary has been the same legal company or remained operational continuously since 1914. This distinction is central to Africa's Enduring Enterprises.

## Evidence timeline

| Date | Event | Evidence | Continuity implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1914 | Commercial origins of the group in Uganda | [Madhvani Foundation](https://madhvanifoundation.com/madhvani-group/) | Group lineage, not a verified incorporation date |
| 1972 | Expropriation and severe operational interruption | [World Bank project assessment](https://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/522471468760552323/pdf/E4700vol060paper.pdf) | Operations were not uninterrupted |
| 1985-05-15 | Kakira Sugar Works (1985) Ltd formally incorporated | [World Bank appraisal](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/546331468175742480/pdf/multi-page.pdf) | New company/legal entity; verify original incorporation records |
| 1989 | Sugar production resumed according to Kakira's company history | [Kakira Sugar](https://kakirasugar.com/about/history/) | Operational recovery; note conflicting descriptions of earlier restart dates |
| 2024-02-26 | Uganda Ministry of Finance reported around 16,000 employees and 40 MW generation, of which 25 MW exported | [Ministry of Finance](https://finance.go.ug/media-center/news-and-updates/hon-musasizi-visits-kakira-sugar-works) | Contemporary scale reported by government, not independently audited |

The World Bank appraisal describes the 1985 joint venture as a 60/40 ownership split between East Africa Holdings Ltd/other shareholders and the Ugandan government. It also describes ownership and operating disputes in the intervening period. **Do not interpret these percentages as present-day ownership.**

## Analytical hypotheses

1. **Family ownership as an institutional memory mechanism:** the family preserved commercial knowledge and relationships across expropriation. This needs interviews and governance records.
2. **Rehabilitation capital as a bridge:** multilateral and development-bank financing helped restart a capital-intensive business; the causal contribution requires historical financing schedules and operational data.
3. **Continuity versus renewal:** corporate longevity should be measured at the level of family, group, individual legal entity and operating asset, separately.
