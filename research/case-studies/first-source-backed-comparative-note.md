# Institutional longevity: three documented case studies

*Research note | 19 October 2026 | Source-backed facts, not a definitive continuity audit*

## Bugisu Co-operative Union (Uganda)

The union's [official history](https://bcultd.co.ug/our-history/) reports that four local unions formed a central union registered in **July 1954**. The predecessor Bugisu Coffee Board dates to **1933** and must not be conflated with the registered union. The union describes an ownership model of more than 300 primary growers' societies, with elected delegates and a central board ([governance account](https://bcultd.co.ug/)). The corporate-registration certificate, uninterrupted operating record and audited finances remain outstanding.

**Institutional question:** Can democratic member ownership sustain capital formation and operational discipline across generations?

## Banque Misr (Egypt)

The bank's [official history](https://www.banquemisr.com/ABOUT-US/History?sc_lang=en) places its establishment in **1920**, under Mohamed Talaat Harb, with initial Egyptian ownership. The bank's [Paris institutional timeline](https://www.banquemisr.fr/notre-histoire.html) records **nationalisation in 1960**. Its [2019–20 annual report](https://www.banquemisr.com/-/media/BM-ANNUAL-DIGITAL-REPORT-2019-2020-en.pdf) states that Talaat Harb resigned on **14 September 1939**.

**Institutional question:** What persisted through the departure of the founder and nationalisation: legal identity, mandate, management capability, capital, or some combination? Original decree and historical accounts remain to be inspected.

## Ethiopian Airlines (Ethiopia)

The airline's [official overview](https://corporate.ethiopianairlines.com/AboutEthiopian/Overview) gives **21 December 1945** as its founding date and **8 April 1946** as the start of operations; it describes the Government of Ethiopia as its **100% owner**. Its [March 2022 announcement](https://corporate.ethiopianairlines.com/Press-release-open-page/mesfin-tasew-appointed-as-new-ceo-of-ethiopian-airlines-group) identifies **23 March 2022** as the effective date of Mesfin Tasew's appointment as group CEO, succeeding Tewolde GebreMariam. Tasew had worked in the airline since 1984.

**Institutional question:** Does the long internal executive pipeline explain part of the airline's resilience? This is a hypothesis, not a causal finding.

## Cross-case observations

- Cooperative member governance, Egyptian-owned bank founding, subsequent state ownership, and state-owned airline succession represent **different mechanisms** of continuity.
- The existence of a company today does not establish uninterrupted operation, legal identity, or profitability.
- Future research should connect each major event to contemporary documents and financial outcomes, not just corporate histories.

**Evidence status:** Official institutional sources support the reported dates and transitions, but independent corroboration and original legal records are incomplete. No enterprise is classified as fully verified under the project's publication standard.
