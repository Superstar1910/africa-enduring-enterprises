## Feature selection

**Working headline:** *A 104-Year-Old African Company Changes Hands: What EABL Reveals About Institutional Longevity*

**Thesis:** The enterprise outlives founders when the legal organisation, brand portfolio, governance, professional management and financing channels are transferable across generations of owners. EABL's prospective Diageo-to-Asahi change of control tests whether those institutional capabilities can persist while local brand ownership, minority investor rights and operating arrangements remain distinct.

**Concise outline**
1. **The founders' company (1922–1936):** the Hurst brothers; KBL origins; exact founding-day conflict to be disclosed.
2. **From brewery to institution (1936–2000):** merger, listing, regional subsidiaries and the transformation of ownership and capital access.
3. **Succession beyond the founder:** Jane Karuku's 2021 internal promotion, distinguishing professional management from ownership.
4. **The ownership test (2025–2026):** contractual mechanics, minority shareholder exemptions, antitrust conditions and closing uncertainty.
5. **Who gets the capital?** Shareholder secondary sale proceeds versus new equity invested in EABL; licensing and control of local brands.
6. **What survives?** Legal identity, governance, operating systems, people, suppliers and capital markets access; empirical indicators to track after closing.

**Caveats:** EABL's 1922 origin is not proof that it was indigenous African-owned at founding. A signed share purchase agreement and merger clearance are not a completed transaction. Governance quality and survival causality cannot be inferred from longevity alone.
